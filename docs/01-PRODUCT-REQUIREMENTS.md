## 1. Vision and positioning

**Vision.** People should know where their money is heading before it gets there, and should be able to trust the advice they're given about it.

**Positioning statement.** For UK earners who get paid once a month and still get caught out before payday (or across the long gap after early December pay), **Foresight** is an agent-native financial intelligence system. Drop in one bank statement and it forecasts your balance for every day of the next 90, simulates decisions before you make them, and proposes interventions that a second agent has checked against your own numbers. Budgeting apps look backwards and chatbots guess. Foresight looks ahead, and **every number it shows has a receipt**.

**The three pillars** (from `00` §1; they map to the judging criteria):

| Pillar | Product promise | Proof the user and judge can see |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast first | "Here is your balance for every day of the next 90, with honest uncertainty." | Forecast fan chart (P10–P90), backtest results in `evals/results/` |
| Advice that is checked | "No recommendation reaches you until the Verifier agent has recomputed it." | Verification history in the Evidence drawer; rejected drafts are visible |
| Every number has a receipt | "Click any figure to see how it was calculated." | `Calculation` records, Evidence drawer, audit events |

**Design stance: single-account first** (`00` §3). One current account with everything flowing through it is the default experience, the primary demo and the judge's path. Multi-account views, joint accounts, the money map and coverage-gap prompts are **progressive layers** that appear only when the imported data contains them. A single account is complete on its own: a forecast never needs a second account.

**What Foresight is not:** a budgeting dashboard with a chatbot attached, a robo-investment advisor, a bank, or an Open Banking aggregator.

## 2. Personas and their problems

All personas, people, accounts and figures are explicitly fictional (`00` §4). They are generated by the seeded persona generator (`09`), which emits files in real export formats. P1 figures follow `00` §10a.

### P1 — Amara Nwosu, 30, QA engineer, Leeds (primary demo and judge path, one account)
- **Situation:** everything flows through **one Monzo current account**. Salary £4,050 net (Bacs) on the 28th, moved to the previous working day at weekends; December pay lands early on Fri 18 Dec, so the next is 28 Jan, a **41-day gap**. Rent £1,250 (standing order, 1st); on the 15th a £400 savings transfer, council tax £162 and about £250 family support abroad via LemFi; energy £78 (20th), water £42 (5th), broadband £31 (9th). Subscriptions: Sky TV £46, PureGym £27.99, Netflix £12.99, Spotify £12.99 and about £31 of others. Balance £2,184.50 as of 11 Oct (declared; Monzo CSVs carry no balance). Buffer £500. No goal set. October turns tight because of a one-off £650 car repair (KwikFit) on 14 Oct.
- **Problems:**
  1. She only notices the pre-payday squeeze when it arrives. Before the car repair her lowest balance before payday is about **£812** (0% chance below her £500 buffer); after it, about **£162 on 27 Oct**, a **100%** chance below the buffer.
  2. Food delivery crept up (**£286.40** in the last 30 days vs a **£203.10** median, **+41%**, 23 orders) and nothing told her.
  3. She can't tell whether a **£1,200 holiday** is affordable. Paying on 6 Nov would take her to about −£148 on 26 Nov; paying after 27 Nov keeps December's low at about £1,086.
  4. She pays for Netflix *and* Sky TV without noticing the overlap.
  5. She distrusts generic tips, and she doesn't want an app suggesting she cut the money she sends her family.
- **Job to be done:** "Tell me early if I'm heading for trouble, and give me one specific thing to do that actually fixes it."

### P2 — Jamie Clarke, 34, freelance motion designer, Bristol (one account)
- **Situation:** one account exported via generic CSV. 2–5 client invoices a month (£900–£6,500). Self Assessment payments on account due 31 Jan and 31 Jul.
- **Problems:** monthly budgets don't fit irregular income. Jamie needs to know how long the runway lasts if a client pays 45 days late, and whether the January tax bill is covered.

### P3 — Kofi & Hannah Mensah, 33/31, Nottingham (multi-bank household; the progressive layer)
- **Situation:** Kofi's salary lands in Starling. Both contribute to a **joint Nationwide** household account (statements as PDF) that pays rent and bills. Kofi uses a Chase account for day-to-day spending, topped up from Starling. An **untracked Capital One** credit card is paid from two accounts. A tracked Tesco Bank credit card (CSV) is settled from Nationwide. None of these banks has a built-in format profile, so each CSV goes through a proposed profile confirmed once.
- **Problems:** single-account apps double-count card bills and transfers, count partner contributions as income, and can't see the Capital One card at all. Bank data can't say *who* spent from the joint account, so advice that blames one partner is wrong and unwelcome. The joint account's statements are only available as PDFs here. The same friend appears under three name spellings.
- **Job to be done:** "Give us one honest picture across our banks, and warn us before the big payments land."

### P4 — Aanya Rao, 27, product designer, Bengaluru (one account, India locale; proves generality)
- **Situation:** one savings account at a fictional Indian bank, exported as **XLS/XLSX** with a balance column. Salary ₹1,15,000 net on the last working day; rent ₹32,000 via UPI on the 3rd; UPI-heavy spending (Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit, Uber); SIP ₹5,000 on the 5th; 7 subscriptions; food delivery ₹14,860 in 30 days vs a 3-month median of ₹10,540 (+41%). Workspace country `IN`, home currency INR, `in` locale pack.
- **Problems:** UPI narrations (`UPI/DR/<ref>/<payee>/<bank>/…`) hide the merchant inside a reference string; amounts use lakh grouping (₹1,15,000); dates are dd-mm-yyyy; Indian statement PDFs are often password-protected; SIPs and EMIs must not be read as discretionary spending.
- **Job to be done:** "Tell me where my money is going and whether I'm on track, in rupees, without me cleaning the export first."
- **Honesty note:** we have no real Indian statements. P4 and the India pack are validated only on synthetic data and a hand-written (constructed, not real) UPI narration holdout, reported separately. No built-in Indian `FormatProfile` is claimed; Indian files go through the generic importers and a proposed profile confirmed once.

## 3. Differentiation and value proposition

| Typical FinPilot submission / market app | Foresight |
|---|---|
| LLM writes the forecast or the numbers in the advice | LLMs never produce a displayed number. A seeded Monte Carlo engine (`finance_core`) computes everything, and the LLM cites `calc_` IDs. |
| A single prompt dressed up as "agents" | A Coordinator plus 5 specialist agents, each with its own prompt, tools and typed output contract, in an explicit LangGraph |
| Advice is generated and shown | Advice is **generated, recomputed, challenged, and revised up to 2 times** before it is shown. The rejection reasons are visible. |
| Monthly budget view | 90-day daily balance with a P10–P90 band, aware of UK payday rules (weekend shifts, early December pay, the long January) |
| Imports trusted blindly | Every import with balances or stated totals passes a **reconciliation gate**; a CSV without them is explicitly marked `not_available`. Text-based PDFs whose layout passes the reconciliation gate are parsed deterministically and rejected with a row-level report rather than silently half-imported. |
| Advice that treats every outflow as discretionary | Commitments like family support abroad are never suggested for cuts. Betting insights are neutral and opt-in. One-offs are excluded from the baseline. |
| "AI accuracy: 95%" in the README | A reproducible `python -m evals.run --suite all` writing committed JSON results, compared against baselines, including a hand-labelled holdout set our own generator didn't produce |
| Static dashboards | Structured generative UI: agents pick from 10 typed components, the server fills them from real calculations, and snapshots reload without re-running the agents |

**Value proposition in one line:** *drop one statement, get a forecast you can interrogate and advice you can audit.*

### 5.1 Ingestion, importers and ledger

There are exactly **two generic importers**, `csv_statement` and `pdf_statement`. Bank knowledge lives in **data**, never in bank-specific code: `FormatProfile` (CSV) and `LayoutProfile` (PDF). Built-in profiles ship for Monzo and Revolut. Unknown banks get an LLM-proposed profile that the user confirms once and that is then reused.

## 7. Functional requirements

Each FR is testable. The test type is noted: **U** = unit, **I** = integration, **E** = eval suite, **UI** = UI/e2e. All monetary thresholds are given in GBP for the UK demo and are configured in the workspace's home currency (minor units per ISO 4217).

### 7.1 Ingestion and importers (FR-01 – FR-12)
- **FR-01** Dropping a CSV ≤ 10 MB / ≤ 20,000 rows or a text PDF ≤ 20 MB / ≤ 60 pages routes it to one of two importers by file type (`csv_statement` or `pdf_statement`). The importer then identifies the bank format by matching a `FormatProfile`/`LayoutProfile` header signature (there is no bank-specific code path), and shows per-file status (profile matched, rows read, date range, reconciliation result) within 3 s for CSV. *(I)*
- **FR-02** `csv_statement` locates tables by scanning for header-like rows (not assuming row 1) and splits multi-table files into one table per account/pocket. Profiles support a signed amount column **or** separate in/out columns; the date formats `dd/mm/yyyy`, `dd-mm-yyyy`, `dd-mm-yy`, `dd.mm.yyyy`, `yyyy-mm-dd`, `d Mon yyyy` and `d Sept yyyy` (date order inferred per FR-78); optional balance, ID and bank-category columns; and skip rules (zero-amount, `Total`, separator rows). *(U)*
- **FR-03** Built-in `FormatProfile`s for Monzo and Revolut are shipped as data files (e.g. `backend/finance_core/importers/profiles/*.yaml`) and validated by fixture tests. Monzo: `Transaction ID` = idempotency key, £0.00 card-check rows skipped, `Category` stored as `bank_category` (a model feature only), no balance column → exactly one "today's balance" gap question or relative mode. Revolut: one account per non-empty currency pocket, summary/`Total`/separator rows skipped, running balance used for anchors and reconciliation. A test asserts that the importer code contains no bank names. *(U)*
- **FR-04** Unknown CSV header → the Ledger Agent proposes a `FormatProfile` from the redacted header plus ≤ 5 digit-masked sample rows (it never outputs amounts or transactions). The user confirms or edits the column roles once, and the profile is saved and reused for that bank. If the proposal fails schema validation twice, the user is shown the manual column mapper. *(U, I)*
- **FR-05** Reconciliation gate: if a file has a running balance, every row must satisfy `balance[i] = balance[i−1] ± amount[i]` (±£0.01), and any stated totals must match Σin/Σout. On failure the import is **rejected** with a row-level report (row, expected, found). There are no silent partial imports. Sparse running balances are checked at date-group boundaries. With no running balance, independently stated totals (or opening/closing anchors) must match. With neither balances nor totals, CSV is accepted with `method=none, status=not_available`; PDF is rejected as unverifiable. *(U, E)*
- **FR-06** Every transaction stores provenance: `import_id`, importer, profile ID and version, source row/page number, raw description and raw amount strings, file SHA-256. *(I)*
- **FR-07** Duplicates (profile-declared ID column as idempotency key; else same account, date, amount and normalised description) are not inserted twice on re-import or overlapping files. *(U, E)*
- **FR-08** Balance anchors come from balance columns (latest per account) or a declared/manual dated balance. The user is never asked for an "opening balance". With no anchor, forecasts run in relative mode and "Add today's balance" is shown. *(I, UI)*
- **FR-09** If the latest anchor is > 7 days old with later transactions, or reconstruction disagrees with a later anchor by > 1%, a "balance may be stale" flag and an "add this month's statement" prompt are shown. *(U)*
- **FR-10** A manual transaction requires date, amount, direction and description. Category is optional (the Ledger Agent fills it). The forecast refreshes within 3 s (P50). *(I)*
- **FR-11** An import batch can be undone. Its transactions are soft-deleted, dependent findings and forecasts are marked superseded, and an audit event is written. *(I)*
- **FR-12** Demo workspaces P1–P3 (and P4 when the `in` pack ships) load in under 10 s from committed generator output and run the full ingestion graph (the agents are really called). *(I)*

### 7.2 Generic PDF statements (FR-13 – FR-17) — Tier 2
- **FR-13** Text-based PDFs: words are extracted with coordinates. The header row is found by synonym sets (Date · Description/Details · Paid in/Money in/Credit · Paid out/Money out/Debit · Balance). Columns are clustered by x-position, wrapped description lines merged, and repeated page headers and footers removed. *(U)*
- **FR-14** If the layout confidence is below threshold, the Ledger Agent receives **only** the redacted header plus ≤ 5 redacted sample rows and returns a `LayoutProfile` (column x-ranges, date format, direction rule). The LLM never outputs amounts or transactions; extraction is deterministic. *(U, I)*
- **FR-15** Direction comes from the columns or, when unlabelled, from running-balance movement. Opening balance = closing − Σin + Σout. A header like "Balance on <first day>" is not assumed to be opening (it may be end-of-day). *(U)*
- **FR-16** Every PDF import passes the FR-05 gate. With no running balance, a totals-only check is applied and rows are marked `confidence=low` with a review prompt. Rejected PDFs produce the row-level report. *(U, E)*
- **FR-17** Password-protected PDFs (common for Indian banks) prompt for the password and decrypt locally in memory. The password is never stored or logged. Image-only PDFs are detected and rejected with "scanned statements aren't supported yet". Confirmed layouts are saved as reusable `LayoutProfile`s. *(I)*

### 7.3 Categorisation and counterparties (FR-18 – FR-26)
- **FR-18** Categorisation order: deterministic rules (incl. UK payment-type codes) → merchant memory → LLM (batched, ≤ 50 txns per call) for anything still below confidence 0.85. *(U)*
- **FR-19** Each transaction stores `category_id`, `confidence`, `method ∈ {rule, memory, llm, user}`, and for LLM labels the `step_` ID. Bank-provided categories are stored as `bank_category` and never copied as the label without the pipeline. *(I)*
- **FR-20** A user correction sets `method=user` and, if the user chooses, writes a merchant-memory entry that applies to future and (optionally) past transactions from that merchant. *(I)*
- **FR-21** The taxonomy is fixed for v1 (canonical list in `07`) and includes `income.salary`, `income.other`, `transfer.own`, `transfer.untracked`, `transfer.from_untracked`, `partner_contribution`, `savings_investment`, `debt.loan`, `debt.bnpl`, `credit_card_bill_unitemised`, `family_support_abroad`, `betting`, `betting_return`, `transfer.reimbursement`, `inflow_unconfirmed` and `uncategorised` (category keys are separate from `flow_kind`). The LLM must return a taxonomy member; anything else becomes `uncategorised` plus a retry. *(U)*
- **FR-22** `family_support_abroad` (LemFi, Wise, WorldRemit, Remitly, etc. to individuals abroad) is a **commitment**: it is forecast as an obligation and excluded from cut proposals (FR-50c). *(U)*
- **FR-23** `betting` and `betting_return` are separate categories. `betting_return` is never income. Betting insights are generated only if the user has opted in, and are worded neutrally (no moral language). *(U, E)*
- **FR-24** Counterparty aliasing: counterparties with name variants (case, initials, truncation, reordered names) are proposed for merge by string similarity plus shared payment patterns. Merges apply only after user confirmation and are reversible. Variants of the user's own display name are proposed as *self*, so those transfers are own-account transfers. *(U, UI)*
- **FR-25** Low-confidence (< 0.6) transactions appear in a review queue offered from Home and Transactions. Onboarding never blocks on it. *(UI)*
- **FR-26** Categorisation accuracy and macro-F1 are measured on the generated set **and** the hand-labelled holdout, against the rules-only and LLM-only baselines. *(E)*

### 7.4 Accounts, income and household (FR-27 – FR-36)
- **FR-27** Each import is bound to one account. Bank and account type are auto-detected from the file where possible. Individual/joint is asked only when a second account or partner-like transfers are present (default individual). *(I)*
- **FR-28** With one account, no multi-account UI is shown: no scope selector, money map or joint prompts. *(UI)*
- **FR-29** Income streams are detected from ≥ 3 recurring credits (cadence ±3 days, amount CV ≤20%; two cycles are a confirmation/declaration candidate only) (source, robust-median net amount, payday rule incl. previous-working-day shift, bank holidays, early December pay, four-weekly cadence). The user confirms or corrects them in "Check what we found". Bonuses and arrears are one-offs excluded from the baseline. Freelance income (P2) is modelled as a distribution. *(U, E)*
- **FR-30** Declared income is accepted only when it isn't observable (untracked account, new job/raise, < 3 cycles). It is shown with a "declared" badge. When observed and declared both exist, the observed figure drives the forecast and a divergence > 10% raises a flag. *(U)*
- **FR-31** `savings_investment` and `loan_repayment` (incl. BNPL instalments, car finance) reduce projected cash and are forecast, but are excluded from spending metrics and anomalies. Refunds, reimbursements and split settlements (confirmed by the user) are excluded from income. *(U, E)*
- **FR-32** Own-account transfers pair on amount ± fee, ±2 days and counter-account hints, and are excluded from income and spend. An outflow to an own but untracked destination is `transfer_to_untracked`. When its 90-day total is ≥ £50, a coverage-gap prompt offers "declare what it pays for" (creates `DeclaredObligation`) or "import that account". *(U, UI)*
- **FR-33** Credit cards: with the card tracked, the bill payment pairs with the card's payment credit and is a transfer (no double count). Untracked, it is `credit_card_bill_unitemised`. Payments to the same card from several accounts are aggregated into **one** gap prompt. *(U, E)*
- **FR-34** Joint account (only if present): the user picks an arrangement (default `treat_as_household_pot`) and must tick partner consent before the import is committed. The consent is stored as an audit event. Partner deposits are `partner_contribution`, never income. *(I, UI)*
- **FR-35** No finding, recommendation or narrative attributes joint-account spending to a person. The Verifier fails any proposal that names or implies the partner as the spender. *(U, E)*
- **FR-36** Forecast scope (only if > 1 account): `Mine` = individual accounts + the user's share of joint flows per the arrangement; `Household` = all tracked accounts; single account. Default `Household` if a joint account exists. Every `fc_` and `sim_` records its scope. *(U)*

### 7.5 Behaviour analysis (FR-37 – FR-42)
- **FR-37** Recurring detection finds series with ≥ 3 occurrences (≥ 2 for annual with prior-year evidence), cadence weekly / four-weekly / monthly / quarterly / annual / seasonal (e.g. council tax 10 instalments Apr–Jan), ±3 days tolerance, amount coefficient of variation ≤ 15%. It outputs the next expected date and amount. *(U, E)*
- **FR-38** Absolute thresholds are same-currency pack/config data (`00` §12.13); £ examples below are UK defaults, never reused as INR/EUR/JPY amounts. Category anomaly: flagged when 30-day spend has robust z ≥ 2.0 against trailing windows **and** the absolute delta is ≥ max(£25, 15% of the median). Non-spend classes, commitments and one-offs are excluded. *(U, E)*
- **FR-39** Subscription detectors flag price changes (≥ 5%) and overlapping services in the same subcategory (e.g. Netflix with Sky TV). They never claim a subscription is "unused" unless the user marked it so. *(U)*
- **FR-40** One-off detector: outflows ≥ 3× the category median with no recurrence (or known one-off classes such as insurance renewal) are excluded from the discretionary bootstrap and listed as one-offs. *(U, E)*
- **FR-41** Long-January insight: when the December pay date is ≥ 7 days earlier than the usual payday, a finding reports the gap length (P1: 41 days) and the projected P50 low before the January pay. *(U)*
- **FR-42** Every finding stores `detector`, parameters, `calc_` references and supporting `txn_` IDs. The Behaviour Analyst's narrative uses value placeholders only; hydrated values must exactly match deterministic formatting of the cited calculations after display rounding, or it is rejected and regenerated. Detection precision/recall is measured on injected anomalies. *(U, E)*

### 7.6 Forecasting and simulation (FR-43 – FR-48)
- **FR-43** The baseline forecast produces daily P10/P50/P90 balances for 90 days, the buffer-breach probability, the minimum-balance date and value (P50), pre-salary lows and end-of-month balances. The same calendar + Monte Carlo model continues beyond 90 days for extended scenarios/goals, labelled lower-confidence (30–180 days normally, up to 365 for a goal). *(U)*
- **FR-44** Reproducible: identical inputs + seed + `engine_version` give byte-identical result JSON. *(U, property test)*
- **FR-45** Each forecast persists an `fc_` record with `data_version`, scope, assumptions (recurring obligations and income streams used, the bootstrap window, excluded one-offs, N paths, seed, anchor provenance) and a `calc_` per derived metric. A new forecast records a diff against the previous one (Δ P50 at 30/60/90 days, Δ breach probability, changed assumptions). *(I, U)*
- **FR-46** `ScenarioSpec` supports the canonical `scenario_spec/1` union in `07` §6.5: `one_off_outflow`, `one_off_inflow`, `split_outflow`, `income_delay`, `income_change_pct`, `recurring_add/change/cancel`, `category_cut_pct`, `category_cap`, `move_to_goal`, `buffer_override`. Modifiers compose. *(U)*
- **FR-47** A simulation reuses the baseline seed, so an empty scenario reproduces the baseline exactly. Adding an outflow never raises any percentile on any date; cutting spend never lowers one. *(property test)*
- **FR-48** Natural-language scenario parsing returns a `ScenarioSpec` plus plain-language assumptions. Ambiguity (e.g. "flights home" with no amount or date) triggers a clarifying question, not a guess. Goal projection returns probability and P50 shortfall/surplus by the goal date, baseline and per scenario. Backtesting uses a rolling origin (7/30/60-day primary horizons; 90 days reported separately) and reports MAE, MAPE, P10–P90 coverage and pinball loss against 2 naive baselines. *(U, E)*

### 7.7 Advice and verification (FR-49 – FR-55)
- **FR-49** The Advisor emits 1–3 `InterventionProposal`s per trigger. Each has an action type from `06` §4.4 (`cap_category`, `cancel_recurring`, `downgrade_recurring`, `defer_purchase`, `move_purchase_date`, `set_aside_amount`, `reschedule_transfer`, `build_buffer`), its parameters, and cited `fnd_`/`fc_`/`goal_` IDs. Its impact is computed by `simulate` and stored as `calc_`; any number in the text must match. *(U)*
- **FR-50** Deterministic Verifier checks, all of which must pass: (a) every cited ID exists and belongs to the workspace; (b) the recomputed impact matches the claimed impact within ±1%; (c) feasibility: no category cut more than 40% below its 3-month median; never cut rent, council tax, utilities, insurance or loan repayments; never cut `family_support_abroad` unless the user asked; a cancelled recurring exists and is active; feasibility must not depend on unconfirmed inflows continuing (V-19); (d) the impact direction improves the target metric; (e) no joint-spend attribution to a person (FR-35); (f) generic-advice check (no cited evidence, or text matches the generic-tip patterns) fails. *(U)*
- **FR-51** The LLM judge (after the deterministic checks pass) scores specificity, grounding and tone, including neutrality on betting (pass/fail with reasons). A fail counts as a rejection. *(E)*
- **FR-52** On rejection, the critique goes back to the Advisor. At most 2 revisions; after that the proposal is `withdrawn`. Every pass is a persisted `ver_` record. *(I)*
- **FR-53** Only versions with a persisted APPROVE verdict are shown as advice (`approved`, then `shown`/`accepted` lifecycle states; UI label “Checked”). Withdrawn ones remain visible in the Evidence drawer. *(UI)*
- **FR-54** Recommendation outcome: after the period ends, the target metric is recomputed and the recommendation is marked `verified_effective` / `not_effective`. *(I)*
- **FR-55** Verifier catch-rate: on the adversarial set of deliberately bad proposals (`09`), the rejection rate is measured and reported per defect type. *(E)*

### 7.8 Orchestration and conversation (FR-56 – FR-63)
- **FR-56** The Coordinator is a LangGraph `StateGraph` with named nodes per agent. System events route by a fixed table. User questions route by LLM intent classification into the `IntentClassification.intent` enum in `06` §4.0 (mapped to response presentation intents by `08` §4.1). *(U, I)*
- **FR-57** Every LLM call goes through `mesh/mesh_client.py` (base URL `https://api.meshapi.ai/v1`, key from `MESH_API_KEY`). No other module imports an LLM SDK (enforced by a test). *(U, CI)*
- **FR-58** Each agent node writes an `AgentStep` (agent, model, prompt version, input hash, output, tokens, latency, cost estimate, status). Each request writes a `run_`. *(I)*
- **FR-59** Retries: a schema-invalid LLM output gets 1 repair retry with the validation error. A transport error gets 2 retries with backoff. After that the node degrades (categorisation → rules-only + `uncategorised`; layout proposal → mapping confirm shown to the user; narrative → template). *(U)*
- **FR-60** Per-run budget (default ≤ 12 LLM calls, ≤ 60k tokens). Exceeding it stops gracefully with a partial response that says so. *(U)*
- **FR-61** AI Workspace responses are `AgentResponse` snapshots of validated blocks. Reloading a thread renders from snapshots without calling agents. *(I)*
- **FR-62** Every block is clickable for follow-up ("Ask about this"), which passes the block's references as context to the next turn. *(UI)*
- **FR-63** Out-of-scope or unsafe requests (investment picks, tax filing, anything not about the user's data) get a scoped refusal with what Foresight can do instead. *(E)*

### 7.9 Proactivity, Home briefing, goals and digests (FR-64 – FR-69)
- **FR-64** A daily 06:30 workspace-local scheduled job (APScheduler, persisted job store) runs: refresh findings → forecast → advisor/verifier on material change → Home briefing regeneration. *(I)*
- **FR-65** "Material change" means any of: a new finding of severity `watch` or `act`, Δ breach probability ≥ 10 pp, Δ P50 at 30 days ≥ 10% or ≥ £100, goal probability dropping across 50%, a new import, or a stale anchor refreshed. *(U)*
- **FR-66** The Home briefing is a persisted `brf_` record. Visiting `/home` reads it (P95 < 300 ms server time) and never calls an LLM. Figures and prose are stored together as an immutable hydrated snapshot; the snapshot is regenerated on material change. Stale reads preserve its values and show “as of”/“Updating…” until the new edition is ready. *(I)*
- **FR-67** The digest contains one key insight and one approved action, with a deep link to the evidence; if no action is approved, it contains an honest status/next step instead. It is sent on a user-chosen schedule (default Monday 08:00 workspace-local time (Europe/London for UK; Asia/Kolkata for India)) by the scheduler, not a button. A "send test digest" control exists but is labelled and logged as a test. *(I)*
- **FR-68** Insight feed events are persisted as typed events: `risk_detected`, `behaviour_changed`, `forecast_updated`, `recommendation_generated`, `recommendation_revised`, `recommendation_withdrawn`, `goal_at_risk`, `intervention_verified`. *(I)*
- **FR-69** Goals: create, edit, archive, with a `funding_rule` (default `residual`: progress = projected pre-salary low at the goal date − buffer). Progress and probability recompute on each forecast. Falling behind triggers the Advisor with the goal as the target metric. *(I)*

### 7.10 Audit and data rights (FR-70 – FR-73)
- **FR-70** Audit events are append-only and record actor (user/agent/system), action, entity, before/after hash and timestamp. Consents, declarations and counterparty merges are audited. *(I)*
- **FR-71** From any displayed recommendation, the Evidence drawer resolves in ≤ 2 clicks: findings, forecast, calculations, supporting transactions with import provenance and reconciliation status, agent steps, and every verification pass. *(UI, E)*
- **FR-72** A user can export all their data (JSON + CSV zip) and delete a workspace (hard delete of workspace-scoped rows including the identifying audit chain; only a non-identifying deletion counter remains). *(I)*
- **FR-73** Every repository query requires a `workspace_id` (via `WorkspaceContext`). A test asserts no repository method runs without one. *(U)*

### 7.11 Workspaces, currencies and locales (FR-74 – FR-85)
- **FR-74** Every workspace has `country`, `home_currency` (ISO 4217), `locale` and `timezone`; the `country` selects a `LocalePack`. Demo workspaces are P1–P3 (`uk`) and P4 (`in`). *(U)*
- **FR-75** Money is stored as integer minor units with the currency's ISO 4217 exponent. Parsing and formatting round-trip exactly for JPY (0), GBP/INR/EUR (2) and KWD (3); no code path assumes 2 decimals. *(U, property test)*
- **FR-76** Each imported account gets a currency by the ladder: currency column → unambiguous symbol/code in amounts or headers → matched `FormatProfile`/`LayoutProfile` → workspace home currency. Ambiguous symbols (`$`, `kr`, `¥`) produce exactly one one-tap confirmation in "Check what we found". *(U, E)*
- **FR-77** Ambiguous separators require H13 confirmation before commit. Decimal and grouping separators are inferred per file (`1.234,56`, `1,234.56`, lakh `1,15,000`, no-decimal yen); a file whose separators are inconsistent is rejected with the offending rows, never silently mis-parsed. *(U, E)*
- **FR-78** Date order (dd/mm vs mm/dd) uses a saved/built-in format or unambiguous dates; otherwise H13 asks once with the workspace locale pre-selected, and nothing is committed until answered. *(U)*
- **FR-79** Forecasts and simulations run per currency. Workspace views show the home currency; accounts in other currencies appear as separate balances with their own forecast; no figure ever sums two currencies. *(U, E)*
- **FR-80** Card spend abroad uses the settled home-currency amount; `original_amount`/`original_currency` are kept for display only. *(U)*
- **FR-81** `LocalePack`s are data files (`uk`, `in`): number/date formats, holiday calendar (python-holidays, versioned), payday conventions, category rules and merchant lexicon, narration parsers, protected categories and wording rules. A test asserts the engine has no country-specific branches outside packs. *(U)*
- **FR-82** The `in` pack parses UPI (`UPI/DR|CR/<ref>/<payee>/<bank>/…`), NEFT, IMPS, NACH/ECS mandates and ATM narrations into payee + payment type; recognises EMI (`loan_repayment`), SIP/RD/FD/PPF (`savings_investment`) and GST/advance tax; shifts paydays using national + state holidays. *(U, E)*
- **FR-83** XLS/XLSX files are routed to `csv_statement`: each worksheet is read as a table, then the same table locator → `FormatProfile` → reconciliation path applies. *(U, E)*
- **FR-84** India categorisation is scored on a separate, hand-written UPI narration holdout labelled `constructed`; results are reported apart from the UK holdout, and the README states that India support has not been validated on real statements. *(E)*
- **FR-85** The Verifier and Advisor apply the workspace pack's wording and protected categories (e.g. no product or fund recommendations under `in`; family support never targeted under either pack). *(U)*

### 7.12 Release and deployment (FR-86 – FR-87) — rev 5

- **FR-86** A feature is visible in the deployed app and claimed in the README only if its flag in `features.yaml` is on, and CI rejects any commit that turns a flag on while one of its linked eval suites is red or missing in `latest.json`. The README's "Built" and "Not shipped (planned)" sections are generated from `features.yaml` + `latest.json`; no claim is hand-typed.
- **FR-87** `main` auto-deploys to the public URL only when CI is green (images are published to GHCR only after a successful CI run on `main`; Watchtower rolls them out on the owner's VPS; `05` §12). Each deploy seeds the demo workspaces from the seeded generator through the real importers, never contains real data, and shows its commit SHA in the app footer; the README links the URL from Stage 0 onward.

## 10. Assumptions

1. Synthetic data is acceptable for judging (confirmed on the official page).
2. UK banks offer CSV export (Monzo, Revolut, Starling and most card providers) or text-based PDF statements. Scanned statements are rare enough to defer.
3. Mesh exposes at least one fast/cheap and one stronger chat model with structured-output support through the OpenAI-compatible API. Exact model IDs are an open decision (`00` §12.1).
4. A 90-day horizon suits salary-cycle decisions. Extended scenarios and goal dates use the same calendar + Monte Carlo model and are labelled lower-confidence beyond 90 days.
5. A single local user with a profile switcher satisfies the user-level data isolation intent for the hackathon.
6. Most users will start with one main account. The multi-account layer is validated on P3 and stress variants.
7. Bank data cannot identify who spent from a joint account; Foresight doesn't try.
8. Judges run evals with their own `MESH_API_KEY`; deterministic suites also run without one.
9. Indian banks commonly export XLS/CSV or password-protected text PDFs. **We have no real Indian samples**; India formats in the generator are our best reconstruction and are labelled as such.
10. Users rarely need cross-currency totals for cash-flow decisions; per-currency forecasts are sufficient for v1.

## 12. Definition of MVP success (measurable)

The MVP is successful when **all** of the following are true on a fresh clone **and on the deployed URL** (items marked T2 apply only if that Stage 4 item shipped; otherwise it must appear under "Not shipped (planned)"):

1. **CI**: all 4 critical Agent Arena checks pass on `main`.
2. **Single-account path**: dropping the P1 Monzo CSV completes the 3 steps (one gap question: today's balance) and lands on Home built from live engine output.
3. **Orchestration**: one P1 run produces a `run_` whose `AgentStep`s include all 5 specialist agents and the Coordinator, with real Mesh calls (non-zero tokens).
4. **Core loop**: the actual graph rejects and revises an explicitly injected bad proposal, and normal P1 runs report observed revisions without forcing a predetermined draft. The adversarial-set rejection rate is *measured and reported* (target 100% for deterministic defect types and ≥95% overall, `09` §9.3; the actual number is reported whatever it is).
5. **Forecast**: backtest results for P1 and P2 committed (P3 when Stage 4(a) ships) (MAE/MAPE, P10–P90 coverage, both naive baselines).
6. **Categorisation**: accuracy and macro-F1 committed for the generated set and the hand-labelled holdout, with rules-only and LLM-only baselines.
7. **Simulation**: property tests (FR-44, FR-47) pass. The £1,200 holiday scenario renders baseline vs simulated, showing the lowest balance for each payment date.
8. **Import integrity**: `csv_statement` fixture tests pass for the built-in Monzo profile (Revolut once Stage 4(a) ships) and for ≥ 2 generic-bank CSVs that go through an LLM-proposed profile. The CSV reconciliation gate (Tier 1, `10` ING-15) rejects or flags all 20 `CSV-CORRUPT` fixtures with a row-level report, and a balance-free file is never labelled "reconciled". (T2: fictional PDFs in ≥ 3 layouts reconcile, with the per-layout pass rate reported.)
9. **Audit**: the audit-completeness eval reports the share of displayed recommendations whose full evidence chain resolves (target 100%; actual reported).
10. **Proactive**: the scheduler sends a digest (Telegram or email) without manual triggering, and an audit event proves it.
11. **UI**: Landing, onboarding, Home, AI Workspace, Decision Lab, Transactions, Activity, the public `/quality` page and the Evidence drawer work against live backend data for P1 **in the deployed app**; any surface not finished is hidden and listed as "Not shipped (planned)" (FR-86).
12. **Multi-account correctness** (T2, only if Stage 4(a) ships): on P3, the tracked Tesco Bank card bill is not double-counted, partner contributions are not income, and the untracked Capital One card produces exactly one gap prompt. All asserted by tests.
13. **README**: what we built, how the agents cooperate, how to run it, key design decisions, the eval table taken from `evals/results/`, the deployed URL, and generated "Built / Not shipped (planned)" sections (FR-86).
14. **Currency generality**: the EUR (comma decimals) fixture and the ambiguity fixtures (Stage 1), plus JPY (zero decimals) and mixed-currency (Stage 4(d), if shipped), import with exact minor-unit round-trips, and the mixed workspace forecasts per currency with no cross-currency sum (asserted by tests).
15. **India (T2, only if Stage 4(c) ships)**: P4 XLS imports through `csv_statement` with a confirmed proposed profile, UPI narrations parse, and India categorisation results on the constructed holdout are committed and labelled synthetic-only.

## 13. Explicitly not built during the hackathon

- Open Banking connections.
- Scanned/image PDF statements (OCR).
- Multi-tenant production auth, organisations, per-member household logins.
- Investment, credit, insurance, pension or tax-position recommendations; no product recommendations of any kind.
- Moving money, paying bills, or any write access to bank accounts.
- Native mobile apps and push notifications.
- Learned ML forecasting models beyond the bootstrap/Monte Carlo engine.
- FX conversion and combined cross-currency totals (accounts in other currencies are imported and forecast in their own currency; no figure sums two currencies).
- Locale packs beyond `uk` and `in`; built-in Indian bank `FormatProfile`s (no real samples).
- Claims of "subscription unused" without a user signal; attribution of joint spending to a person.
